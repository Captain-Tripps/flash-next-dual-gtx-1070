# LGA 3647 socket — showcase render (design)

Date: 2026-11-17. Owner: Ranger 1. Status: awaiting Jon's approval.

## Intent and why
A single HTML file that shows an LGA 3647 CPU socket off as a **showcase render**:
it should look like a beauty shot of a real part, not a CAD screenshot. Jon said
"showcase", so visuals over measurement accuracy.

## Goals
- One self-contained `index.html`. No network, no build step, no CDN.
- Gold land-grid on the ceramic substrate, in a correct LGA 3647 layout
  (75.9 × 61.4 mm land-grid envelope, pad rows, central opening in the field).
- Retention/ILS bracket and a load plate that physically **hinges open** on its
  pivot, with latch lever, on a real animated cycle.
- Slow rotating camera (auto-orbit, no interaction required), with user controls
  as a bonus.
- Material look: ceramic substrate, gold/soft-gold contacts, black-anodized frame,
  plated steel bracket. Contact-shadowing and specular are what sells this.

## Non-goals
- Datasagram-accurate dimensions. True proportions and correctly placed features,
  not 0.1 mm tolerances.
- A CPU model, PCB, socket pin internals, or pin-count correctness at the wire level.
- Export/print/tessellation tooling.

## Anti-goals
- A flat-looking scene with only ambient light (the #1 cause of "cheap" WebGL).
- 3,647 individually-modeled meshes: no. That is a draw-call budget blow-up.

## Constraints
- Zero dependencies, one file. Hand-written WebGL 2.
- Has to be readable at 60 fps on a laptop iGPU: instancing, not per-pad geometry.

## Approaches considered
1. **Hand-written WebGL 2, zero deps.** Fully offline, one file, full control over
   materials. Cost: I write the orbit camera and shader myself. Chosen.
2. **three.js from a CDN.** Orbit controls + PBR for free. Rejected: breaks the
   "one file, no network" constraint Jon's request implies.
3. **CSS 3D / DOM per pad.** Would not survive 3.6k elements and looks nothing like a render. Rejected.

## The how, as decided by me
- **Scene graph in JS, not a library.** A tiny entity/component layer: parts are
  named nodes with a transform; each part is built from extruded/profile-based
  primitives generated in code (no external model files).
- **Pads: one instanced draw call.** A pad is a rounded micro-plane (2 triangles +
  a chamfer), instanced ~3.6k times with per-instance offsets. The central cutout
  in the field is generated by the layout function, not painted.
- **Lighting:** two area-ish lights + a rim light, a cheap screen-space-ish
  contact shadow under the plate, and a Fresnel/gold BRDF. Gold is deliberately
  multi-tone: pads slightly rougher than the bracket's plating.
- **Animation:** load plate runs a timed cycle — closed → latch flips → plate
  rotates open around its hinge axis → holds → closes. Plate geometry is a child
  of a hinge node, so the rotation is about the real pivot, not a faked spin.
- **Camera:** slow continuous orbit with a slight elevation drift, plus drag to
  orbit / wheel to zoom, plus keys to open/close the plate.
- **HUD:** minimal — a title, part labels on the parts, and an on-screen state
  readout (cycle phase, camera angle). No control panel sprawl.

## What's left to the builder
Exact shader tuning, which keyframes read well at orbit distance, and whether the
pad field gets a per-row shimmer. All visual polish, no functional change.

## Verification
End-state assertions, not symptom checks: drive to a known pose and assert the
plate's hinge angle, pad instance count, camera angle, and a pixel probe that gold
pixels exist in the frame. Screenshot the finished frame, not a mid-flight one.
